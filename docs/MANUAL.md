# The Clearing: a short manual

The Clearing is a page for writing, inside Hermes. You write. When you want a second pair of eyes, an editor gives you one note at a time, pinned to your exact words. The editor never types into your page.

## Open it

Click **The Clearing** in the Hermes sidebar, or press Cmd K (Ctrl K on Windows and Linux) and choose **Open The Clearing**. The first time, a sample piece opens with an offer to run a Quick check on it.

## Write

- The title is the top line of the page. Press Enter in the title to drop into the text.
- Your words save on every pause. "Saved" shows in the top line.
- The top line fades while you type and comes back when you stop or move the pointer.
- The word count sits beside the title.

## Get a note

There are three ways in.

1. **Quick check.** Press Cmd E (Ctrl E), type "quick", press Enter. Or use Quick check this piece in the Cmd K palette. It is built into The Clearing and works without your model. It marks throat-clearing, stock openings ("In today's rapidly changing world"), stacked hedges, filler, stock phrases, "not X, it's Y" setups and long sentences. It leaves words inside quotation marks alone, because you can't edit a quote. Its first note says what it found and that these are questions, not rules: keep anything that sounds like you.
2. **The Ask line.** Press Cmd E (Ctrl E) and say what you want in plain words, like "my ending is weak", "where do readers get lost", "spelling", "nut graf", "subtext" or "the middle sags". Pick a match with the arrow keys and Enter. The pass runs on your own Hermes model.
3. **Other words.** Double-click any word, or put the cursor in it and press Cmd E (Ctrl E): Other words is the first row. A small card opens beside the word with its meanings and other words for it, from the built-in dictionary. Click one to swap it in.

To work on part of a piece, select it first. The pass then reads only that passage, and the note says "your selection". When you select with the pointer, a small bar appears just above it: **Rewrite**, **Tighten**, **Make it concrete** and **Ask**. Cmd E with a selection offers the same three first.

## Work through the notes

Each note sits beside the words it is about, and those words are underlined in your accent color.

- Each note names one change and says why. Most carry a **Try**: the passage rewritten to show that change, in your own register. It is a suggestion; use it, adapt it or ignore it.
- **Show me** (the first button when a note carries its change) puts the change into your sentence on the page, washed and underlined so you can read it in place. **Use this** keeps it; **Back to mine** leaves your words. When a note has no Try, Show me asks your model to draft one.
- **Edit it myself** (the last link when a note has a Show me) puts your cursor at the start of the passage, with nothing selected, so the first key you press adds to it instead of replacing it. Change what you like.
- **Leave it** moves on without changing anything.
- **Done** closes the notes. The marks go away.
- After the last note, **Read the summary** shows the editor's overall read. **Walk the notes** goes through them again from the first, underlined.

If you type elsewhere while the editor is working, each note still lands on its own words. If you rewrite the passage itself, the note for it is set aside. It is never placed on the wrong words.

## Suggested text

Your model writes suggested rewrites, next sentences, endings and titles. Each goes into your page when you click Use this, and you can turn suggested text off in The room.

- **Rewrite a passage**: select a sentence or a paragraph, then **Rewrite**, **Tighten** or **Make it concrete** in the bar above it. A, B and C fill in as each arrives, each labeled with the move it makes; **Yours** is always last. Click one to see it in the page. **Edit first** lets you change it before it goes in. **Use this** puts it in; **Yours** or **Done** leaves your words.
- **Change it**: under every option and every Try, **Shorter**, **Plainer**, **Keep my rhythm**, or type how you want it changed, for three new versions (the last round stays one click back). **Why?** answers in two or three plain sentences.
- **Ask the editor in your own words**: Cmd E, type what you want ("make the ending land harder"), Enter. The first row always sends your words as they are. With a selection you get three rewrites; without one, notes pinned to your piece, each with Show me.
- **Suggest a next sentence**: stuck? Put the cursor where the next sentence should go and press Cmd E (Ctrl E): a next sentence is the first row. Three ways the next sentence could go, in your voice (and your voice samples, if you marked any). Where a sentence needs a fact your piece doesn't have, it leaves a blank in brackets for you.
- **Propose endings** and **Title ideas**, alongside Propose openings.
- Suggested words show as a proofreader's mark while you try them: your words struck through, the suggestion beside them. After you use them they keep a faint wash and a tick in the margin until you type inside them, and the top line says how much of the piece is your own words. **Undo** takes a suggestion back out: the button on the card right after you use it, or Undo in the top line, beside Redo (Cmd Z and Cmd Shift Z do the same). A change that moves the news to the top and cuts its repeat is one step, so one Undo takes out both. A version of the page is kept before each one goes in, and History lists **Suggested text you used**, with the model that wrote it.
- Suggestions that would bring a stock phrase, filler, a stacked hedge or throat-clearing into your piece are left out before you see them. If every one would, The Clearing asks once more; if they still would, it shows them anyway, each with the habit it adds.
- Every card your model wrote ends with "Suggested by your model". Point at it for the model's name; History names it too.
- **Turn it off**: title menu, **The room**, **Suggested text: Off**. Your model then points and asks but drafts no words: no rewrites, next sentences, openings, endings, titles, or Try in its notes. Quick check, other words and the editor's notes still work. Good for a class that wants a room with no AI-written words.

## Other passes that propose text

You always decide.

- **Fix punctuation and typos** and similar passes bring change records. **Keep** or **Skip** each one, then **Apply** the ones you kept.
- **Propose openings** gives three openings, A, B and C. Click one to see it in your page. **Yours** brings your own back. **Keep** makes it yours; **Go back to mine** leaves your opening alone.
- **Try a shorter draft** strikes through what the piece could lose. Click any struck passage to keep it. **Accept the cuts** removes the rest. Your longer draft is kept as a version first.
- **Suggest wording** asks your model for four other ways to say a word, phrase or sentence, with what each one changes.
- **Three ways to state the main point** and **Three ways to make the promise** work like openings, on the sentence that carries your point or your promise. If your piece has no such sentence yet, the options are tried after your first paragraph.
- **Turn material into a draft** turns your notes (selected, or the whole page) into three drafts. Anything the notes don't give is listed beside the draft, never invented. A number or a quotation that is not in your notes is flagged before you keep it. Keeping a draft saves your notes in History first.

## Methods with their own card

- **Check whether it sounds like you** needs at least one piece marked as a voice sample (title menu, **Use as a voice sample**). Each note says whether the passage sounds like your samples, drifts from them, or there isn't enough to tell, and quotes the sample it rests on.
- **Get several editorial perspectives**: a newcomer, a skeptic and a line editor read the piece separately. Walk each one's notes, or **Where they agree** for passages more than one of them marked. If one reviewer fails, the others still show.
- **Let reviewers argue it through** starts from the panel (it runs one first if needed), names the decision in front of you, lets each side make its case and answer the other, then says what moved and what is still open. It never names a winner.
- **Keep a lesson for next time** reads your own revisions to this piece and the suggestions you kept or skipped, and proposes a few narrow rules. **Build your writing guidance** proposes rules from your voice samples. Edit a rule before you keep it, or skip it.
- **Find the piece across drafts**: choose one to four other pieces. The editor reads them together and names the piece they are trying to become, with the passages that carry it, the gaps and the decisions left to you.

## Your guidance

Title menu, **Your guidance**. The rules you kept, by area (voice, style, audience, guardrails, workflow). The editor reads them on every pass. Edit or remove any rule, or write your own. **Set up a writing home** writes your guidance, voice samples and pieces into a folder of plain files. You see the plan first; a file already in the folder is never changed.

## Bring words in

Click **Bring in** in the top line.

- **A Word file**, **a PDF**, **a text or Markdown file**: each becomes a new piece. Only the words come in; pictures, comments and layout stay in the original. A PDF that is a scan (pictures of pages) or locked with a password says so.
- **A link**: paste the address of a public web page. Your Hermes web tool reads it. **Make it a new piece**, or **Add it at my cursor**. Either way it comes in as clean paragraphs under one line saying where it came from. Addresses on your own machine or private network are refused.
- **Talk it through**: or click the microphone beside the Ask line. Speak, then click it again to stop. Your words land at the cursor. The Clearing uses the speech-to-text you set up in Hermes (Settings, Voice). Bring in says where your words go: "Typed out privately. Nothing is sent." when the speech-to-text runs on your own machine, or "Sent to OpenAI to type out" (or whichever service), and then The Clearing waits for **Send** before your recording leaves. The first time, macOS asks whether Hermes may use the microphone.
- **Paste text**: paste, then make it a new piece or add it at the cursor.

## Other words

Click a word in the card to swap it into the page. Your original word and every word you tried stay in the card, so you can go back with one click. **Suggest wording** in the same card asks your model for options that fit the sentence.

## The title menu

Click the title in the top line.

- **New piece**, **Open a file** (.md, .txt, .docx or .pdf) and your five most recent pieces
- **History**: every saved version of this piece. **Restore** brings one back. What is on the page now is kept as a version first. History also says how many editor passes ran on this piece and about how many words your model read and wrote for them.
- **The room**: the scene (Snow Light, Quiet Marsh, Night Tide, a plain page, or **Your own picture**: a JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 6 MB, which you can change or remove), larger text, stronger contrast, and **Suggested text** shown or off. Every scene stays your choice in light and dark mode; dark mode only deepens the veil over a light picture.
- **Show the shape**: one bar per paragraph, with a tick where notes are. Click a bar to go to that paragraph.
- **Move to trash** and **Trash**: nothing in the trash is deleted. Restore it any time.
- **Use as a voice sample**: mark a piece you wrote yourself, so the voice check and your guidance can learn from it.
- **Your guidance** and **All methods**: every pass, method, craft path and figure by group, each one click away. Each craft path says who it is for. Writing fiction? **Find the human story** is for fiction, memoir and personal essays: scenes, stakes, the turn, the ending, whose story it is.
- **About The Clearing**: what The Clearing does, where your words go when you ask the editor, and software credits.

## Finish

Click **Finish** in the top right. Press Escape to close this or any other sheet.

- **Copy as Markdown** or **Copy as plain text**
- **Save as .md** or **Save as .txt** into a folder you choose
- **Save as Word** opens Hermes's save dialog
- **Mark this version finished** keeps a version you can find in History

## Choose the editor's model

The Clearing uses your main Hermes model unless you pick another. To pick one: Settings, Models, Auxiliary, **The Clearing editor**.

## When something goes wrong

Every problem shows a plain message in the margin, and your words stay as they were.

| You see | What to do |
|---|---|
| Hermes doesn't have a model ready | Click **Open Settings** and choose a model. Quick check and Better words still work. |
| Your model is busy | Wait a minute and try again. |
| Your model took too long | Try again, or select one section and run the pass on that. |
| The editor stopped before finishing | Select a shorter section and try again. |
| The Clearing's saving is off | Turn The Clearing on in Settings, Plugins. Your words are kept on the page until saving works. |
| The Clearing can't hear the microphone | Allow Hermes in System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, then try again. |
| Dictation needs speech-to-text | Turn on voice in Hermes Settings, Voice. |
| Bringing in links needs a web tool | Set one up in Hermes Settings, Tools, Web. |
| That PDF has no text to read | It is probably a scan. Bring in a version with selectable text. |
| Your model's suggestions leaned on stock phrases | Try again; The Clearing left them out so they never reach your page. |
